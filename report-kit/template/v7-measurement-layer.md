# 19. Your measured operating system {.newpage}

> **The next upgrade is not another identity lens. It is a measurement loop: evidence → work-design decision → seasonal re-check.**

Version 6 already has enough convergence to name the direction: **Human-Centric Operations Architect**. The useful question now is not “who else might I be?” It is “what conditions let me do that work sustainably, well, and without paying for it later?”

This section replaces the earlier open-ended test list with four measurements that answer that question. None has been completed for this version, so the status is deliberately **baseline pending**. No score is assumed and no conclusion is upgraded until the result exists.

| Measure | Status in V7 | What it actually settles | Decision it will inform |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Work Design Questionnaire (WDQ)** | Baseline pending | The task, knowledge, social and contextual features of work that energise or drain you | The non-negotiables in a role, contract, project brief or future consultancy offer |
| **HEXACO-60** | Baseline pending | The six major personality dimensions, including Honesty–Humility, which Big Five does not measure | Where ethics, boundaries and client-trust patterns are genuine strengths—and where they need structure |
| **Adult ADHD Self-Report Scale (ASRS-v1.1)** | Baseline pending | A self-reported screen for current adult attention and executive-function symptoms | Which supports should be engineered into the work system; it is not a diagnosis |
| **Copenhagen Burnout Inventory (CBI)** | Baseline pending | Personal, work-related and client-related burnout at one point in time | Whether the work design is sustainable across event seasons, not merely survivable during them |

The WDQ is the missing career system. It measures 21 work characteristics across task, knowledge, social and contextual categories, making it a better bridge between personality insight and real job design than another typology label.[1] The HEXACO-PI-R adds Honesty–Humility to the existing Big Five picture.[2] The ASRS-v1.1 is included only as a screening and support-planning tool; a result does not diagnose ADHD or replace clinical assessment.[3] The CBI gives three separate burnout lenses, so intense client work is not confused with a general lack of capacity.[4]

## The working operating brief — pending measurement

The table below is a **provisional build brief**, drawn from V6’s measured strengths, existing work history and recurring working hypotheses. Complete the four measurements before treating any row as final.

| Work condition | Current V6 signal | Provisional rule until measured | What a good project looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Defined scope and visible ending** | Strong preference for order; burnout risk when care has no boundary | Every engagement needs an outcome, owner, deadline and close-out | A production phase, systems-diagnosis sprint or clearly bounded retained advisory role |
| **Autonomy in the design phase** | High autonomy need; strongest work is diagnosis and structure | Negotiate the method, not only the deliverable | “Here is the outcome. You design the workflow and decision path.” |

| **Recovery designed into the programme** | Event-season intensity and self-preservation risk | Recovery days, invoicing and debriefs are booked work—not rewards | A load-out handover followed by protected recovery and an explicit next-step decision |

<p class="note">This is a work-design document, not a verdict on personality, health or worth. A measurement only earns authority when it changes a practical decision.</p>

# 20. Baseline protocol and decision rules {.newpage}

> **Treat the four baselines like pre-rig checks: they reduce avoidable strain before the show goes live.**

Complete the measures in two short sessions, not as a weekend of self-analysis. The aim is a usable baseline, then a decision—not perfect self-knowledge.

| Session | Complete | Capture only | Time box |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Session A — personal operating data** | ASRS-v1.1 and HEXACO-60 | Date; source link; score or result summary; one sentence on what surprised you | 20 minutes |
| **Session B — work and strain data** | WDQ and CBI | Date; top three energising conditions; top three strain points; baseline burnout scores | 30–40 minutes |
| **Integration review** | Compare results with this report | Three rules to keep; one rule to test for 30 days; one role or client filter | 20 minutes |

## How the results become decisions

| If the measurement shows… | Do this in the next role, project or contract | Do not conclude… |
|---|---|---|
| **High autonomy, task variety or task significance on the WDQ** | Protect ownership of the diagnostic/design phase and select projects with a visible human outcome | That you cannot work within a team or process |
| **High social-support or feedback need on the WDQ** | Put a named decision-maker, weekly check-in and written feedback point into the brief | That needing clarity is a weakness |
| **An ASRS screen that raises concern** | Reduce unnecessary task-switching; use visible next actions, body-doubling, deadlines and an external capture system; discuss it with a qualified clinician if you want assessment | That a screener has diagnosed you or set a ceiling on your capability |
| **High CBI score or a material rise after a project** | Change workload, recovery, boundaries or client mix before accepting more intensity; repeat at the next event-season checkpoint | That you have failed or need to “try harder” |
| **High Honesty–Humility on HEXACO** | Write scope, payment triggers and change-control rules before goodwill becomes unpaid labour | That integrity requires self-erasure or undercharging |
